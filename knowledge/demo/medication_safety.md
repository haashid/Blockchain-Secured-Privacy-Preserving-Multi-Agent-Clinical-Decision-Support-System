# Medication Safety in Clinical Practice

## Common Drug Interactions

### ACE Inhibitors + Metformin
- ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril) may enhance the hypoglycemic effect of metformin
- Monitor blood glucose more frequently when starting or adjusting ACE inhibitor dose
- Risk is generally low but clinically relevant in elderly patients

### ACE Inhibitors + Potassium-Sparing Diuretics
- Risk of hyperkalemia
- Monitor serum potassium regularly
- Avoid combination in patients with renal impairment

### NSAIDs + ACE Inhibitors
- NSAIDs reduce the antihypertensive effect of ACE inhibitors
- Increased risk of acute kidney injury
- Avoid concurrent use when possible

### Metformin + Alcohol
- Increased risk of lactic acidosis
- Advise patients to limit alcohol consumption
- Hold metformin before procedures requiring contrast dye

## Common Allergy Considerations

### Penicillin Allergy
- Cross-reactivity with cephalosporins: approximately 1-2%
- Higher cross-reactivity with first-generation cephalosporins
- Consider penicillin skin testing for patients labeled penicillin-allergic
- Most patients labeled penicillin-allergic can tolerate penicillins upon formal testing

### Sulfa Drug Allergy
- Cross-reactivity between sulfonamide antibiotics and non-antibiotic sulfonamides is debated
- Avoid sulfonamide antibiotics (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, sulfasalazine)
- Non-antibiotic sulfonamides (celecoxib, furosemide, thiazides) may be used with caution

### Drug Rash with Eosinophilia and Systemic Symptoms (DRESS)
- Severe drug hypersensitivity reaction
- Common culprits: anticonvulsants, allopurinol, sulfonamides, minocycline
- Characterized by rash, fever, lymphadenopathy, eosinophilia, organ involvement

## Medication Monitoring

### Metformin Monitoring
- Renal function (eGFR) at least annually
- B12 levels periodically with long-term use
- Hold before iodinated contrast procedures
- Resume 48 hours after contrast if renal function stable

### ACE Inhibitor Monitoring
- Serum potassium within 1-2 weeks of initiation
- Renal function within 1-2 weeks
- Blood pressure response
- Cough (common side effect, occurs in 5-20% of patients)

### Lisinopril Specifics
- Starting dose: 10 mg daily for hypertension
- Maximum dose: 40 mg daily
- Onset of action: 1-2 hours
- Peak effect: 6 hours
- Duration: 24 hours

## Polypharmacy Considerations

Patients taking 5 or more medications are at increased risk for:
- Drug-drug interactions
- Adverse drug reactions
- Medication non-adherence
- Falls (in elderly)
- Cognitive impairment

### Beers Criteria
The American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria lists medications potentially inappropriate for older adults:
- NSAIDs (increased GI bleeding, renal impairment)
- Benzodiazepines (increased fall risk, cognitive impairment)
- Anticholinergics (confusion, urinary retention)
- Sulfonylureas (hypoglycemia risk)
