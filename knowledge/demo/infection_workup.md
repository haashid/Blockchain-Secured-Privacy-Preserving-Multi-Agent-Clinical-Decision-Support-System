# Infection Workup in Febrile Patients

## Overview

This document provides clinical guidance for the workup of febrile patients presenting to the emergency department or inpatient setting.

## Fever Definition

Fever is defined as a core body temperature of 38.0°C (100.4°F) or higher. Persistent fever refers to fever lasting more than 48 hours despite initial evaluation.

## Initial Workup

### Laboratory Tests

1. **Complete Blood Count (CBC)**
   - White blood cell count (WBC): Elevated WBC >11,000/μL suggests bacterial infection
   - WBC <4,000/μL may indicate severe infection or bone marrow suppression
   - Left shift (increased bands) suggests acute bacterial infection

2. **Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP)**
   - Assess renal function (creatinine, BUN)
   - Electrolyte abnormalities may indicate sepsis

3. **Blood Cultures**
   - Obtain at least 2 sets from different sites
   - Before starting antibiotics when possible
   - Positive blood culture confirms bacteremia

4. **C-Reactive Protein (CRP)**
   - Elevated CRP >50 mg/L suggests significant inflammation
   - CRP >100 mg/L often indicates serious bacterial infection

5. **Procalcitonin**
   - >0.5 ng/mL suggests bacterial infection
   - >2.0 ng/mL suggests sepsis
   - Useful for antibiotic stewardship

6. **Lactate**
   - >2.0 mmol/L in the context of infection suggests sepsis
   - Serial lactate measurements guide resuscitation

## Sepsis Screening

### qSOFA Criteria (Quick Sepsis-Related Organ Failure Assessment)
- Respiratory rate ≥22/min
- Altered mentation (GCS <15)
- Systolic blood pressure ≤100 mmHg

Two or more criteria suggest high risk for poor outcome.

### SOFA Score
The full SOFA score assesses:
- Respiratory (PaO2/FiO2)
- Coagulation (platelets)
- Liver (bilirubin)
- Cardiovascular (MAP, vasopressors)
- CNS (GCS)
- Renal (creatinine, urine output)

## Antibiotic Timing

- Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour of sepsis recognition
- Delayed antibiotic administration increases mortality by approximately 7.6% per hour

## Diabetic Patients with Infection

Diabetic patients are at increased risk for:
- Urinary tract infections
- Skin and soft tissue infections
- Invasive fungal infections
- Necrotizing fasciitis

Blood glucose should be closely monitored during infection as stress hyperglycemia is common. HbA1c >8% indicates poor glycemic control which impairs immune function.
