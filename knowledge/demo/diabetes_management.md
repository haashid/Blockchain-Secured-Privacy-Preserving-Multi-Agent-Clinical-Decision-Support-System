# Diabetes Mellitus Management in Clinical Practice

## Type 2 Diabetes Overview

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by insulin resistance and progressive beta-cell dysfunction. It affects approximately 10% of the adult population.

## Diagnostic Criteria

- Fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL
- HbA1c ≥6.5%
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL during OGTT
- Random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms

## HbA1c Targets

- General target: <7.0% for most non-pregnant adults
- Less stringent target (<8.0%): elderly patients, those with comorbidities, history of severe hypoglycemia
- More stringent target (<6.5%): young patients, short disease duration, no significant CVD

## Pharmacotherapy

### First-Line
- **Metformin**: 500-2000 mg/day
  - Reduces hepatic glucose production
  - Weight neutral or modest weight loss
  - Contraindicated if eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²
  - Monitor B12 levels with long-term use

### Second-Line Options
- **SGLT2 inhibitors** (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin)
  - Cardiovascular and renal benefits
  - Risk of genital mycotic infections
  - Monitor for euglycemic DKA

- **GLP-1 receptor agonists** (semaglutide, liraglutide)
  - Cardiovascular benefit
  - Weight reduction
  - GI side effects common

- **DPP-4 inhibitors** (sitagliptin, linagliptin)
  - Weight neutral
  - Well tolerated
  - Modest efficacy

- **Sulfonylureas** (glipizide, glyburide)
  - Risk of hypoglycemia
  - Weight gain
  - Inexpensive

- **Thiazolidinediones** (pioglitazone)
  - Risk of heart failure exacerbation
  - Weight gain
  - Bone fracture risk

## Insulin Therapy

Indicated when:
- HbA1c >10% at diagnosis
- Symptomatic hyperglycemia
- Failure of oral agents
- During acute illness or surgery

## Monitoring

- HbA1c every 3-6 months
- Self-monitoring of blood glucose
- Annual eye examination
- Annual foot examination
- Regular renal function monitoring (eGFR, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio)

## Complications

### Acute
- Diabetic ketoacidosis (more common in T1DM)
- Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS) - more common in T2DM
- Hypoglycemia (medication-related)

### Chronic
- Cardiovascular disease (leading cause of mortality)
- Diabetic nephropathy
- Diabetic retinopathy
- Diabetic neuropathy
- Peripheral arterial disease

## Infection Risk

Diabetes significantly increases infection risk:
- Urinary tract infections (2-3x increased risk)
- Skin and soft tissue infections
- Pulmonary infections
- Fungal infections

Impaired immune function in diabetes is related to:
- Poor glycemic control
- Neutrophil dysfunction
- Impaired chemotaxis
- Reduced phagocytosis
